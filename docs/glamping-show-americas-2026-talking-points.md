# Glamping Show Americas — five lines to remember

**Aurora, CO · September 28–30, 2026**

Say the bold sentence. Use one proof point if they lean in. Stop there.

Sage figures below are the US market-overview cohort (published, private commercial, operating glamping) as of the latest inventory update on **September 18, 2026**: **536 open properties**, **7,358 units**, property-level median retail rate **$226** (mean **$266**).

---

### 1. Glamping is the part of outdoor hospitality still growing

**Say:** “Traditional parks are flat to down this year. Glamping resorts are the segment still growing.”

- Campspot same-store site nights through May 2026: glamping resorts **+10.9%** year over year. The median park was **−1.2%**. Parks with at least 20% of inventory in cabins, cottages, or glamping were the only site-type segment still up.
- The global market is about **$4.1 billion in 2026**, growing near **10% a year** (Fortune: $4.07 billion, 10.7% CAGR to 2034; Grand View: about $4.1–4.2 billion, 9.5% CAGR to 2033). North America is the fastest-growing major region.
- Sage tracks **536 open US properties** on that book. That is the number to say.

**If they push:** Research firms disagree by a few hundred million dollars on the global total. Quote “about $4 billion, growing about 10% a year.”

### 2. Guests are paying more and staying longer

**Say:** “ADR is up, stays are longer, and discounting to fill sites is the move that is not working.”

- Glamping Show Americas 2025: premium glamping ADR **$251**, up from **$207 in 2023**. Average stay lengthened from **2.2 nights (2024) to 2.7 (2025)**.
- Campspot Q2 2026: occupancy **44.5%** (down 1 point) while ADR rose **3%**. September–December 2026 occupancy on the books is pacing **3–4% ahead** of last year.
- On Sage’s book the typical open US property sits at a **$226** median retail nightly rate.

**If they push:** $251 is the premium-industry average from that 2025 session. OHI’s campground-glamping median is about **$107 midweek and $117 weekend**. Say “premium glamping” when you use $251.

### 3. Occupancy holds as price rises, until the extreme top

**Say:** “Moving from a $150 night to a $400 night does not empty the calendar. It multiplies revenue per site.”

- Sage / Hipcamp US cabins, 2025 (June analysis): occupancy stays about **48–52%** from under $100 through the **$400–600** band. RevPAR in that band is about **3×** the $100–199 band. Only about **4%** of priced cabin listings sit there. Median occupancy breaks once rates go above $600.

**If they push:** Hipcamp skews smaller and independent. Treat that occupancy as a floor for a full-service resort, and pair it with a local comp from Sage.

### 4. Hard walls carry the shoulder. A private tub is a smaller add.

**Say:** “Canvas fills summer. Hard-walled units are what hold a rate into fall and winter.”

- Sage, June 2026, US cabins: Midwest cabins still post real winter rates (about **$180–$200** weekday/weekend). Weekend premiums are only about **9–11%** in every season. Canvas product often closes.
- Current retail rates on the overview (September 18): safari tents are **26%** of open units at a **$373** unit-weighted rate; cabins are **17%** at **$278** (property median **$236**). Domes are **5%** of units and the highest unit-weighted rate in that set, **$394**.
- Private in-unit hot tub, where the tag is an explicit Yes or No: **$339 vs $318**, about **+$21**. 549 units tagged Yes, 4,145 tagged No. Inside safari tents and cabins alone, the tagged-with average is lower, so leave that comparison off the floor.

**If they push:** The June cabin figure of about $415 was a seasonal effective rate (peak-season columns averaged in). The number on the market-overview screen is the retail rate, **$278** unit-weighted for cabins. A shared property hot tub shows no rate gap in the current tag set.

### 5. Hotel brands arrived. The market is still fragmented.

**Say:** “Marriott, Hyatt, and Hilton are in glamping now. The five best-known brands are still only about 12% of North America. Most projects are still local, and they still need local comps.”

- Marriott bought Postcard Cabins (December 2024). Hyatt allied with Under Canvas (July 2024). Hilton partnered with AutoCamp (February 2024).
- Postcard, Under Canvas, AutoCamp, Huttopia, and Tentrr together were about **12%** of the North America market in 2025 (GM Insights).
- Direct bookings are still about **55%** of global glamping revenue. OTAs are growing faster: about **10% a year** globally and about **14%** in North America (Grand View).
